Eaton Fuller 18-speed

A twin-countershaft manual transmission built from two working sections: a five-speed front box and a four-ratio auxiliary section. Explore the outside first, then open the case to see how torque travels through the gear train.

01 / Front section

Five forward positions: LO, 1st, 2nd, 3rd and 4th. The input drive gear feeds two countershafts, which drive the selected mainshaft gear.

02 / Auxiliary section

Range and splitter combine to provide four auxiliary ratio states. The LO front-section position is used only in low range.

03 / Total ratios

Eighteen forward speeds and four reverse speeds. The same main-box ratios are reused across low and high range, with splitter steps between them.

How the architecture works

  • Engine torque enters through the input shaft and main drive gear.
  • The main drive gear drives both front countershafts; their gears mesh with the mainshaft gear stack.
  • A sliding clutch couples the selected mainshaft gear to the mainshaft.
  • Torque passes into the auxiliary section, where splitter and range gearing establish the final ratio.
  • The auxiliary output shaft sends torque to the driveshaft.

What we'll make interactive next

  • Select LO through 4th in the front box.
  • Switch low/high range and splitter states.
  • Animate only the gears and shafts carrying torque.
  • Show the 18 forward and 4 reverse paths with direction and relative speed.
